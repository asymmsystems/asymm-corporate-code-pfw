CascadePlan Guide

CascadePlan User & Administrator Guide

CascadePlan is a timeline inside a Jira Cloud project. Work can start when its predecessor is part done, for example at 30%. When a date moves, the work that depends on it moves too. You see every Jira change before it happens, and you can undo it. This guide shows how to set it up, how to use each screen, and what to do when something goes wrong.

1. Who this guide is for

  • Planners and delivery leads who schedule Jira work and keep dates up to date. Read sections 3 to 9.
  • Plan approvers who manage plans, freeze baselines and accept or reject scope changes. Read sections 7 and 8.
  • Jira administrators who install the app and choose the approver groups. Read sections 2, 11, 12 and 15.

CascadePlan keeps Jira as the record. A dependency is a Jira "Blocks" link. A parent is a Jira parent. An extra person on a task is a Jira sub-task. Every change is a Jira write that you make, with your own Jira permissions.

2. Install and first setup

Install (Jira administrator)

  1. In Jira, open Apps > Explore more apps, search for CascadePlan, and choose Get app. You can also start from the Atlassian Marketplace listing.
  2. Accept the app's access. CascadePlan asks to read and write Jira work, to read users, to store its own data on your site, and to report personal data to Atlassian. It calls no server outside your Atlassian site.
  3. Each project now has a CascadePlan Planner page in its navigation.

Choose the plan approvers (Jira administrator, optional)

Project administrators and Jira administrators are always plan approvers. To add more people, add Jira groups:

  1. Open Jira settings > Apps > CascadePlan approvers.
  2. Type part of a group name in Find Jira groups to grant authority and choose Search.
  3. Choose Add next to each group. Choose Remove to take a group off the list.
  4. Choose Save changes. The page shows "Saved." and the summary line names the groups.

The list holds at most 20 groups. A search shows at most 20 groups, and the search text can be at most 100 characters. The groups apply to every project on the site.

Jira permissions your users need

CascadePlan adds no permission of its own for planning. It uses the Jira permissions of each user (section 11). A planner usually needs Browse projects, Edit issues, Schedule issues, Link issues and Create issues in the project.

Licence

When the site's CascadePlan licence is not active, the planner shows "A CascadePlan licence is required on this site. Ask your Jira administrator." and does not load the plan. The approvers page still works.

The bar toolbar holds Move 1 week earlier, Move 1 week later, Edit dates, Add dependency and Set progress.

4. How CascadePlan works

  • Dates. A bar ends on the work item's Jira due date. Its start is stored on the work item as the issue property cascadeplan.dates, because many projects have no start date field. Every plan and every user sees the same dates.
  • Links with a trigger percent. A link says "B starts when A reaches N%". It is a Jira "Blocks" link. The percent is stored on B in the issue property cascadeplan.links. 100% is a classic finish-to-start link.
  • The cascade. When a move makes a successor start too early, CascadePlan moves the successor, and its successors in turn. You see the list before you drop.
  • Parents follow their children. A task or story with sub-tasks spans its sub-tasks. Its Jira due date follows their end. An epic is moved by moving its work items.
  • Progress and status. The Jira status says whether work has started or is done. Progress says how far work in progress has got. A Done item always shows 100%.
  • Target. Target is the progress the work should have by today, from its dates.
  • Your permissions. CascadePlan checks your Jira permissions before it writes. If one is missing, it changes nothing and names the permission.

5. Schedule and change work

Put a work item on the timeline

  1. Choose Backlog in the toolbar. The tray Unmapped Project Issues opens.
  2. Drag a card onto the timeline at the start date you want. Or choose + Schedule on the card: the bar starts today and spans 3 weeks.
  3. If the item has a Jira due date, the bar ends on that date. If the due date is before the start, CascadePlan refuses and names the due date.

Result: a message says "Scheduled KEY to timeline." The start date is saved on the work item. CascadePlan writes a due date only when you choose an end.

To take a work item off the timeline, choose Move to backlog in the row menu or in the task dialog. Its Jira due date stays as it is.

Create work

  • + Task opens Create Jira Task & Schedule: Summary, Parent Epic, Assignee, Effort Hours (optional) and Start Week. Choose Create & Schedule. CascadePlan creates a Jira task (or the project's task, story or item type) with a 3-week span. Result: "Task KEY created and scheduled."
  • + Epic opens Create Jira Epic. Type the Epic Summary and choose Create Epic.
  • The row button ➕ adds an inline row. Type the summary and, if you want, the hours, then press Enter.

If a create gets no answer, CascadePlan says the result is unknown. Make the same request again: CascadePlan does not create the work item twice.

Move or resize a bar

  1. Drag a bar to move it, or drag an edge to change its start or end. You can also use the bar toolbar or the keyboard (section 10).
  2. Before you let go, read the preview line. It lists every Jira change the drop will make: each due date, each start date, and each epic that will not move.
  3. Let go. CascadePlan saves the change and moves the successors.

Result: a message names the change and offers Undo. A cascade is saved in steps of 25 successors; the planner continues on its own.

Edit dates opens a small editor with Start Date, End Date, -1 Wk and +1 Wk, the duration, Full details... and Save Dates.

A task or story with sub-tasks moves as a block. Sub-tasks not started move by the same days. A sub-task in progress keeps its start. A finished sub-task stays where it is. A block of more than 500 changing sub-tasks is refused.

Add a dependency

  1. Click the bar of the work item that waits, and choose Add dependency. The popover Starts when opens.
  2. Set Starts when that issue reaches (percent). The default is 100. It changes in steps of 5.
  3. In Issue it waits for, choose the predecessor, then choose Add dependency.

Result: CascadePlan creates a Jira "Blocks" link and saves the percent. The successor moves to its earliest start. If Jira refuses the link, CascadePlan adds nothing and says so.

Change or remove a link

Click a link's arrow or its label. The link editor shows the predecessor and the successor, the percent, and whether the trigger is met.

  • Change Starts when predecessor reaches (percent), or choose 0%, 30%, 50% or 100%, then choose Save.
  • Swap direction turns the link around.
  • Remove Link deletes that Jira link, whoever created it.

A link between a work item and its own parent or sub-task is refused, because a parent's dates come from its children. A link that makes a loop is refused too.

A link marked "not in Jira" exists only in this plan. Open it and choose Save to create it in Jira for every plan, or Remove Link to drop it.

Find out why a work item starts when it does

Choose ❓ on a row, or select a row and press ?. The popup Why KEY starts on DATE names the link that sets the earliest start and the trigger, or says the item has no dependency. When the item starts too early, the popup offers Move KEY to DATE or Lower the trigger to N%.

Dependency conflicts

If you drop a bar before its links allow, and Enforce deps is off, the dialog Dependency Conflict names the predecessor and offers:

  • Snap to Earliest Start: move the bar to the first allowed day.
  • Adjust Trigger to N%: lower the percent so the new date is allowed.
  • Keep with Violation: keep the date. The bar shows that it starts too early.
  • Cancel Move: change nothing.

With Enforce deps on, CascadePlan refuses a start before the link allows and asks you to turn the option off to move it anyway.

Change the parent or the work type

  1. Drag a row's handle (⠿) onto the middle of another row. Or open the task dialog and use Parent in Jira (changes every plan) and Apply.
  2. CascadePlan asks first in a Jira message, for example "Move K under P? This changes the parent in Jira, so every plan shows it." Choose Move or Convert.
  3. Turning a task into a sub-task, or back, is a Jira bulk move. The planner shows "Jira is converting K: n%."

Result: "<change>: done in Jira." with Undo. Jira's rules apply: an epic cannot move under another work item, a sub-task cannot have children, and a task with sub-tasks cannot become a sub-task.

Dropping a row on the top or bottom edge of another row reorders it. Row order belongs to the plan.

When progress and the Jira status disagree

When a new progress does not match the Jira status, CascadePlan asks before it saves. For example "PAY-12 is To Do in Jira. Also move it to In Progress?" with Move to In Progress and Keep To Do. For several rows it asks once: Change the statuses or Keep the statuses. Jira's workflow decides whether the move is allowed. If Jira refuses it, the progress is still saved and the Changes panel says why. Escape cancels the whole action.

Change several rows at once

Press Space on a cell other than Start and Due to select a row. Shift extends the selection. The selection bar shows "n selected" and offers Set progress, Move by days, Set assignee and Clear.

7. Plans and plan filters

Each project has a Main Plan. Plan approvers can add scenario plans, for example "Plan B: launch without wallets".

  • Switch: choose a plan in the list. "Switched to plan NAME."
  • Create: type a name (1 to 80 characters) in New plan name... and choose + Create. With Clone current plan tasks & settings ticked (the default), the new plan copies the open plan.
  • Copy, rename, delete: use the row buttons. Delete asks "Confirm?" for 4 seconds; click again to delete. The Main Plan cannot be renamed or deleted. Deleting a plan deletes its baselines and requests.
  • Filter: type JQL in Work items in this plan, for example component = "Payments", and choose Save filter. CascadePlan says about how many work items match. Clear the field and save to read the whole project again.

What each plan owns: row order, columns and widths, hand-set progress and targets, the filter, and baselines. What every plan shares: dates, links, parents and every Jira field. A move in a scenario plan is a real Jira change.

8. Baselines and scope reviews

Choose Baseline in the toolbar. The panel Baselines and scope changes opens.

  1. Freeze (plan approver). Type a name, for example "Sprint 7 commitment", and choose Freeze the plan as a baseline. Result: "Froze "NAME": N items, H h." The baseline records each item's Jira estimate, start, end and done state, with a SHA-256 digest.
  2. Watch the scope. The Baseline button shows the change since the active baseline. The panel lists each item added, removed or changed.
  3. Request (any project user). Write the reason in Why the scope changed (at most 1,000 characters) and choose Request approval of this scope.
  4. Review (plan approver, not the requester). Under Scope-change requests, choose Accept or Reject. Accept makes a new active version named "<name> (version n)". A request can be reviewed once.
  5. Compare and export. Under Baseline versions: Compare with now, Compare with active, Export CSV and, for approvers, Make active. The CSV has the columns issue_id, issue_key, estimate_hours, start, end, done and a SHA-256 comment line.

If a stored baseline no longer matches its digest, the panel warns: "this baseline no longer matches what was frozen". There is no button to delete a baseline.

9. Views, Changes and Undo

Table view

Plain Table View lists every work item with Key, Summary, Type, Epic, Assignee, Start Date, Due Date, Progress, Status and Dependencies. It suits reading and screen readers.

Utilization view

Hours per person per week shows 8 weeks from the Monday of this week. Hours come from the Jira estimates on the plan, spread evenly over each task's days. No capacity is assumed. Epics, unassigned tasks and tasks with no estimate are left out.

Changes

Changes opens Jira writes in this session: Issue, Field, Old, New and Result. A result reads "Written" or "Not written" with the reason. The list covers your session only.

Undo and Redo

  • Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes (Cmd on macOS). The planner keeps your last 20 changes.
  • Undo covers date moves, scheduling and unscheduling, block moves and changes to several rows. Parent and type changes have their own Undo in the message.
  • Creates, plan management, baselines, filters and row order have no Undo.
  • The undo history is lost when you reload the page or switch plans.

11. Permissions

Plan approvers are project administrators, Jira administrators and members of the groups on the CascadePlan approvers page. Only they create, copy, rename and delete plans, set plan filters, freeze baselines, make a version active, and accept or reject scope changes. CascadePlan checks this on the server. Only a Jira administrator can open the approvers page.

ActionJira permission CascadePlan checks first
Open the plannerNone by the app. Jira shows only the work items you can browse.
Change dates, move or resize barsEdit issues and Schedule issues on every work item that changes. A successor you may not change is skipped and listed.
Add, change or remove a linkEdit issues and Link issues
Change a parentEdit issues
Turn a task into a sub-task, or backEdit issues, Move issues, Create issues, and the global permission "Make bulk changes"
Add a sub-task from the dialogEdit issues and Create issues on the parent
Allocate a person (sub-task)Edit issues on the parent; Jira checks the create
Create a task or an epicNone by the app; Jira checks Create issues
Reorder rowsEdit issues on the moved row
Request a scope review, change columnsAny user of the project

If the permission check itself fails, CascadePlan changes nothing: "CascadePlan could not check your Jira permissions, so nothing was changed. Try again."

12. Data stored and where

  • Where: CascadePlan runs on Atlassian Forge. It stores its data in the Forge storage of your site. It sends nothing outside your Atlassian site.
  • In Forge storage: plan records (dates, row order, hand-set progress and targets, estimate unit, contributors), plan settings (columns, widths, filter), the plan list, baselines, scope-change requests with their reasons, and the approver groups.
  • On your Jira issues: the issue properties cascadeplan.dates, cascadeplan.links and, on items CascadePlan created, cascadeplan.create. CascadePlan also makes the ordinary Jira writes you choose: due dates, estimates, assignees, links, parents, sub-tasks and transitions.
  • In your browser: the open plan, the zoom, hidden columns, the hint, Enforce deps and Keys. These stay in your browser only.
  • Personal data: the Atlassian account ids of who froze a baseline, who requested or reviewed a scope change, each person on a task through a sub-task, and the Jira administrator who last saved the approver groups. Names are read from Jira each time and never stored. CascadePlan reports these ids to Atlassian at most once every 7 days, and removes the ids of closed accounts.
  • Retention: records stay until the work item or the plan is deleted. Deleting a work item in Jira deletes its CascadePlan records in every plan.

The privacy policy states the same, for all four apps.

13. Limits

  • A plan reads at most 1,000 work items, newest first. Use a plan filter for larger projects.
  • Plan names: 1 to 80 characters. Plan filter: one line, at most 1,000 characters, no ORDER BY.
  • Approver groups: at most 20. Contributors per task: at most 20. Sub-tasks per change: at most 50.
  • A cascade is saved 25 successors per step; the planner stops after 40 steps. A block move saves 25 sub-tasks per step, and at most 500.
  • Baseline name: 80 characters. Reason and comment: 1,000 characters.
  • Undo: the last 20 changes of the session.

A failed write shows a red message titled "CascadePlan: not saved". It stays until you choose Dismiss. Still stuck? Write to support@asymm.systems.

15. Uninstall and data removal

  1. A Jira administrator opens Jira settings > Apps > Manage apps, finds CascadePlan, and chooses Uninstall.
  2. The planner and the approvers page disappear.
  3. Atlassian keeps the app's Forge storage for 28 days and then deletes it: plans, baselines, requests and settings.
  4. What CascadePlan wrote into Jira stays in Jira: the issue properties, the Blocks links, sub-tasks, due dates and estimates. They are ordinary Jira data.

To delete a plan's data before then, a plan approver deletes the plan. For other requests, write to privacy@asymm.systems.
