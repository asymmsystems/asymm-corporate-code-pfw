LedgerSeal Guide

LedgerSeal User & Administrator Guide

How to seal approved Jira issues, detect and attribute unauthorized post-approval modifications, reseal tickets with audited justifications, configure workflow transition gates, and export compliance evidence.

1. The Approved Ticket Problem

In standard Jira, a developer submits an issue, a product lead or release manager reviews and approves it, and the ticket moves toward release. However, between approval and production deployment, ticket parameters often change: descriptions are altered, acceptance criteria are edited, or architectural attachments are replaced. Jira records that an edit occurred in ticket history, but native Jira cannot prove whether what was built and deployed matches what was approved.

LedgerSeal solves this by introducing cryptographic snapshot seals. When an authorized lead approves a ticket, LedgerSeal captures a canonical SHA-256 hash snapshot of covered fields, comments, and attachments, signs it using an Ed25519 digital signature, and binds it to an immutable hash chain.

2. Sealing a Jira Issue

To seal an issue:

  1. Open the target Jira issue in your project.
  2. In the right-hand panel sidebar, locate the LedgerSeal Audit Gate panel.
  3. Review the covered fields displayed in the panel (Summary, Description, Acceptance Criteria, Attachments).
  4. Click Seal Issue.
  5. Select your approval declaration meaning (e.g. Approved for Release, Reviewed & Accepted, Security Sign-off).
  6. Click Confirm & Seal.

The lozenge turns green [SEALED], displaying the approver account, timestamp, and unique cryptographic certificate ID.

Figure 1: Issue panel in Sealed state with approver attribution and certificate ID.

3. Tamper Detection & Diff Attribution

If any user modifies a covered field, edits an acceptance criterion, adds or deletes an attachment, or modifies covered comments after the seal is applied:

  • The LedgerSeal panel immediately turns amber [CHANGED] or red [COMPROMISED].
  • The panel highlights the exact field that was altered (e.g. description, acceptance_criteria).
  • The author of the change and the exact timestamp are recorded.
  • A visual diff view compares the approved snapshot against current ticket state.

Figure 2: Tamper detection alert attributing unauthorized modifications after approval.

4. Resealing with Audited Justification

When changes after approval are intentional and legitimate (e.g. minor typo fix, updated deployment parameter per CAB):

  1. An authorized approver who is not the author of the edit reviews the diff (enforcing Separation of Duties).
  2. Click Reseal Issue.
  3. In the mandatory justification box, provide the rationale for the change (e.g. Approved revised timeout threshold per CAB-892).
  4. Click Confirm Reseal.

LedgerSeal appends a new block to the ticket hash chain, preserving the previous seal, the change incident, and the new seal in an unbroken audit trail.

5. Workflow Transition Gate (The Validator)

To prevent unsealed or tampered issues from accidentally reaching production, LedgerSeal provides a Jira Cloud Workflow Validator:

  1. Navigate to Project Settings > Workflows and edit your active workflow.
  2. Select the transition into your release state (e.g. In Review or Done).
  3. Click Validators > Add validator.
  4. Select LedgerSeal Approval Validator from the list.
  5. Configure validation rules (e.g. require state to be SEALED, reject TAMPERED or UNSEALED).
  6. Publish the workflow draft.

If an engineer attempts to transition an issue that has been altered without resealing, Jira displays a clear blocking message preventing the transition:

6. Administrator & Policy Settings

LedgerSeal adheres to the Asymm Uniform Permission Management Standard. Jira administrators can define which Jira groups or project roles possess approval authority:

  • Administer: Jira System Administrators and Project Administrators can modify approver groups and covered field sets.
  • Approve: Designated release managers, QA directors, and tech leads can apply seals and authorize reseals.
  • View: Developers and auditors can view seal lozenges, diffs, and cryptographic evidence.

Figure 4: Admin policy configuration specifying authorized approver groups.

7. Exporting Standalone Evidence

Auditors do not need to log into Jira to inspect approval evidence. In the LedgerSeal issue panel, click Export Audit Evidence (JSON) to download a standalone cryptographic evidence file.
