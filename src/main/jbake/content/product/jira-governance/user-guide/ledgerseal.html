LedgerSeal Guide

LedgerSeal User & Administrator Guide

LedgerSeal records the approval of a Jira issue as it is at that moment. If the issue changes after approval, LedgerSeal shows what changed, who changed it and when. A workflow validator can block a transition until the issue is sealed and unchanged. Signers can also type a single-use code that LedgerSeal emails to them, sign on the transition screen, or sign the stages of an approval route. Auditors can check the evidence offline with the open verifier.

1. Who this guide is for

  • Approvers (release managers, product leads, change boards) who seal issues and approve changes. Read sections 3 to 5 and 7 to 9.
  • Team members who need to know why an issue shows CHANGED or why a transition is blocked. Read sections 3, 6 and 14.
  • Auditors who check the evidence. Read section 10 and the open verifier guide.
  • Jira administrators who install the app, choose approver groups and add the validator. Read sections 2, 6 to 9 and 11.

2. Install and first setup

  1. Install (Jira administrator). In Jira, open Apps > Explore more apps, search for LedgerSeal and choose Get app. The app asks to read Jira work and users, to write issue properties, to store its own data, to report personal data to Atlassian, and to send Jira notifications (send:notification:jira). It uses the last one only to email a signer their own signing code, in projects where code signing is on. It calls no server outside your Atlassian site.
  2. Choose approvers (optional). Project administrators and Jira administrators can always seal. To add more people, add Jira groups in Jira settings > Apps > LedgerSeal settings, tab Approver groups (section 11).
  3. Add the gate (optional). Add the validator Require Valid LedgerSeal to the transitions that must wait for approval, for example into "Ready for release" (section 6). Without it, LedgerSeal reports changes but blocks nothing.
  4. Choose how people sign (optional). Turn on code signing for chosen projects (section 7), add the signature field to a transition screen (section 8), or define approval routes (section 9).

Every issue then shows an Approval seal entry in its details column. The full panel opens from the issue's app button: choose Approval Seal.

Licence. When the licence is not active, the panel shows "Approval seal: LedgerSeal licence is inactive." No seal, approval, revoke or export works. The validator still decides; its block message then asks to renew the licence or to remove the validator.

3. The Approval Seal panel and the details entry

The panel has a header with the state lozenge and Refresh, a summary with the state and the next step, the action buttons, and the collapsed section Certificate & attestation details.

4. Seal an issue

  1. Open the issue and the Approval Seal panel. Review the issue.
  2. Choose Seal this issue. Only approvers see this button.
  3. Choose the role of your sign-off: Approved (the default), Reviewed or Verified.
  4. Read the declaration and tick I agree to the declaration above.
  5. Choose Sign and seal.

Result: the lozenge turns green SEALED: "Approval seal: Sealed. No change detected since approval." The history has one entry, "Sealed", with your name and the time. If the issue changed after you opened the panel, LedgerSeal refuses: "The issue changed since you opened it. Nothing was saved. Refresh, review the issue, and seal again."

What a seal covers

The seal covers the issue's fields, its comments, its attachments (names and details, not file content) and its work logs. Fields that Jira changes without a user edit are not covered: for example status, Rank, Sprint, created and updated times, votes, watchers and fields derived from work logs. Open Show fields in the details to see the covered fields and the reason each other field is not covered.

Restricted comments and work logs. A comment or work log restricted to a role or group is covered only when LedgerSeal can read it. Users who cannot see a restricted comment see "A restricted comment that you cannot see in Jira", with no text. The evidence record is exported only to a user who can see every such comment and work log.

5. When an issue changes after approval

LedgerSeal checks the issue when it is edited, commented, given an attachment or a work log, and each time the panel or the validator reads it. Jira events can arrive up to a few minutes late.

Each changed item shows Was (at approval), Now (current edit), Changed by and the time. A rich-text field names the changed part, for example "(Paragraph 1 changed.)". Long values have Show full value. An attachment added, removed or replaced after the seal is a change row too, for example "Attachment: build.log (Added after the seal)". The row shows the file name, the kind of change and, where Jira records it, who made the change and when. The validator message names "Attachments".

Approve the changes

  1. An approver who did not make any of the changes opens the panel and reviews them.
  2. Choose Approve these changes.
  3. Choose the sign-off role, write a reason (1 to 500 characters), and tick the declaration.
  4. Choose Sign and seal.

Result: the history gains "Sealed again", and the panel shows "Sealed. Resealed after 1 change incident." The earlier seal stays in the history.

Separation of duties. A user who, after the seal, changed a covered field, added or edited a comment or work log, or added an attachment cannot approve, revoke or reseal. The panel tells them: "You changed this issue, so another approver must approve it." Changes to fields that are not covered do not count.

Revoke a seal

Choose Revoke seal, write a reason in Reason for revoking (required), and choose Revoke. The panel shows REVOKED with the reason and who revoked it. To seal again after a revoke, the form asks for a reason.

6. Gate a transition (Jira administrator)

  1. Open the workflow in the Jira workflow editor.
  2. Select the transition that must wait for approval.
  3. Add the validator Require Valid LedgerSeal. It has no options.
  4. Publish the workflow.

The validator reads the live issue on every transition, including what is typed on the transition screen. The transition goes through only when the issue is sealed and unchanged. Otherwise Jira blocks it and shows one of these messages:

  • "LedgerSeal: this issue is not sealed. To proceed: open the LedgerSeal panel on this issue, select your sign-off role, agree to the declaration, and click "Seal this issue"."
  • "LedgerSeal: N field(s) changed after approval (names). Ask a project admin who did not make the change to approve it in the LedgerSeal panel."
  • "LedgerSeal: this issue changed after approval and was changed back. Ask a project admin to approve it again in the LedgerSeal panel."
  • "LedgerSeal: the seal was revoked on DATE (reason: ...). Seal it again to continue."
  • "LedgerSeal could not read the issue from Jira, so this transition is blocked. Try again in a minute."

The messages say "project admin", but Jira administrators and members of the approver groups can approve too.

Comments on the transition screen. A comment typed on the gated transition's screen is new content on a sealed issue, so the validator blocks the move with "Comment added". Add the comment after the transition, or ask another approver to approve it first. A resolution set on the screen does not block.

To sign in the transition dialog itself, see section 8. For a route of approval stages, see section 9.

7. Code signing

With code signing on, each signature also needs a single-use code that LedgerSeal emails to the signer's own Atlassian email address. The code is the second component, next to the signer's signed-in Atlassian account.

Turn it on (Jira administrator)

  1. Open Jira settings > Apps > LedgerSeal settings and the tab Signing codes.
  2. Tick Code signing on in these projects.
  3. Type the project keys, separated by commas (at most 100).
  4. Set Lock a person out after this many failed attempts in a row. The default is 5; you can choose 3 to 10.
  5. Choose Save. Each save makes a new version of the settings and adds an alert on the Security alerts tab.

Jira outgoing email must be on. LedgerSeal sends the code through Jira, so when Jira cannot send email, nobody can sign in a code-signing project.

Sign with a code

In a code-signing project, every seal, approval of changes, revoke, stage signature, rejection and signature on the transition screen needs a code.

  1. Open the form as usual, for example Seal this issue, Approve these changes or Revoke seal.
  2. Choose Send me a code. The form shows "We emailed a code to your Atlassian email address. It works once, until" and the time. The email subject is "LedgerSeal signing code for" and the issue key.
  3. Type the 6 digits in Code from the email (6 digits).
  4. Read the declaration and tick I agree to the declaration above. An approval, a rejection and a revoke each show their own declaration, and the signature records it.
  5. Choose Sign and seal (or Revoke, Sign or Reject).

The code works once, for 5 minutes, only for you, that issue and that action. Send a new code asks for another one.

Lockout and unlock

After 5 failed codes in a row (or the number the administrator chose), LedgerSeal locks code signing for that person: "Code signing is locked for you after too many failed attempts. Ask a Jira admin to unlock it." Another Jira administrator unlocks the person on the Security alerts tab: type a reason in Reason for unlocking (required) and choose Unlock. An administrator cannot unlock themself.

Security alerts

The Security alerts tab lists the people locked out and the open alerts, newest first: failed signing attempts, a used code tried again, lockouts, too many code requests, a code Jira could not send, and changes to the code-signing settings. Choose Acknowledge to close an alert. No email is sent for these alerts, so check this page. Each alert and each acknowledgement is kept in a hash-chained record.

What code signing does not do

  • LedgerSeal does not ask for the Jira login again at each signature. The first component is the existing Jira session.
  • A shared or delegated mailbox defeats the code. Each signer needs a personal mailbox.
  • Security alerts are shown on the admin page only. They are not emailed.

8. Sign on the transition screen

Set it up (Jira administrator)

  1. Add the field LedgerSeal signature to the transition screen of the gated transition. Use a screen with no other covered field: a field or a comment typed there changes the approved content, and the validator blocks the move.
  2. Add the validator Require Valid LedgerSeal to that transition (section 6), and publish the workflow.

Sign and move the issue

  1. Select the transition. Jira opens the transition dialog with the LedgerSeal signature field.
  2. In the field, choose the sign-off role, read the declaration and tick I agree to the declaration above. If the issue changed after approval, write a reason. In a code-signing project, choose Send me a code and type the code.
  3. Choose Sign in the field. The field shows "Signed. Select the transition button to finish. The signature can be used once, within 2 minutes."
  4. Select the transition button. The validator writes the seal, then checks the issue as usual.
  • The board, the issue view and the list view open the same dialog.
  • On an issue that is sealed and unchanged, the field says that no signature is needed.
  • On an issue with an approval route, the field says: "This issue has an approval route: sign its stages in the LedgerSeal panel, then move the issue."
  • REST, automation and bulk changes have no screen. The validator blocks them unless the issue is already sealed. Jira Service Management approval steps skip validators.
  • A seal made before canonical form 2.3.0 shows a change the first time the field is set on that issue. Approve the change once.
  • Sign with LedgerSeal in the issue's actions menu opens the signing panel. It works wherever the field cannot be used.

New validator messages:

  • "LedgerSeal: this signature expired or was already used. Sign again in the LedgerSeal field."
  • "LedgerSeal: the person moving the issue is not the person who signed. Sign again."
  • "LedgerSeal: fields on this screen changed the approved content. Use a screen with only the LedgerSeal signature field, or sign in the panel."

What Jira Cloud cannot do

  • An app cannot ask for the Atlassian password again at the transition. There is no API for it, and apps must not collect it.
  • An app cannot intercept the transition button, or open its own dialog before Jira's transition dialog.
  • An app cannot add a field to a transition screen. A Jira administrator must add the signature field.
  • UI modifications do not work on Forge custom fields, on the legacy transition dialog, or in team-managed projects.
  • An app cannot disable the dialog's submit button. Only the validator can refuse the transition.
  • Nobody can sign on a transition run by REST, automation or a Jira Service Management approval step, because there is no screen. The validator blocks the first two; approval steps skip validators.
  • A post function cannot roll back a transition.

9. Approval routes

An approval route is a set of stages that sign in order, for example QA, then Regulatory. Each stage has its own approver groups and quorum. Every stage signs the same content, and the last stage seals the issue.

Define a route (Jira administrator)

  1. Open Jira settings > Apps > LedgerSeal settings and the tab Approval routes.
  2. Choose New route. The route is JSON; the page starts from an example. For each stage, give a stageId, a name, an orderGroup (stages of the same group sign in any order; group 2 starts after group 1), a sign-off role, the approverGroupIds, a quorum ("ANY", or a number of distinct people) and the gatesStatusIds: the target statuses that this stage gates.
  3. Optional: reapprovalStageIds names the stages that must sign again after a change to a routed seal. Without it, the full route signs again.
  4. Choose Save. Each save makes a new version. An approval that is already running keeps the version it started with.
  5. Choose Bind this route, type the Project key and an issue type id, or * for every issue type of the project, and save the binding.

Sign a stage

  1. Open the Approval Seal panel. It shows the route, each stage, its quorum, who signed, and which stages are open, for example "In approval: 1 of 2 stages complete."
  2. Choose Sign as and the stage name. You see this button only for open stages whose groups you are in.
  3. Read the declaration, tick it, and choose Sign. In a code-signing project, send and type a code first.
  • Reject asks for a reason (Reason for rejecting (required)). A rejection blocks the gated transitions until the approval starts again: "Rejected. The next signature starts the approval again."
  • One person signs one stage of an approval.
  • A change to the covered content during approval starts the approval again: "The issue changed during approval, so the approval starts again."
  • When the last stage reaches its quorum, the issue is sealed.
  • Revoking a routed seal needs a member of a group of the last order group.
  • Find issues still in approval with the JQL ledgersealApproval = "OPEN".

Validator messages for routes:

  • "LedgerSeal: this move needs the STAGE approval (k of n signed). Sign in the LedgerSeal panel."
  • "LedgerSeal: STAGE rejected this issue on DATE (reason: ...). Start the approval again in the LedgerSeal panel."

10. Certificate details and the evidence record

Choose Show details in Certificate & attestation details:

  • Signed by and the time, the Sign-off role and the reason, if any.
  • Tamper-evident seal: the SHA-256 hash of the covered content. Show full hash shows all of it.
  • Covered fields: Show fields lists covered and not covered fields.
  • History: Show history lists Sealed, Change found, Sealed again and Revoked entries, and on a route the stage signatures and rejections, 50 per page, with Show more history.
  • Evidence record: Show evidence (JSON).
  • The footer names who can seal, the certificate id, the canonical form and the declaration version.

Give the evidence to an auditor

  1. Choose Show evidence (JSON). The panel shows the evidence record.
  2. Copy the text into a file, for example PAY-29-evidence.json. There is no download button.
  3. The auditor runs node verify-evidence.mjs PAY-29-evidence.json with the open verifier.

The record holds the sealed snapshot, the hash-chained history, the attestation and its Ed25519 signature. A Marketplace installation signs with the key ls-prod-2026-11, listed on the Signing keys page. The record names people by a pseudonymous token, and adds an unsigned map from token to account id.

An issue signed with a code or through an approval route exports the format ledgerseal-evidence/2. It adds the code-signing settings in force, the route versions and the declaration texts. Check it with verifier 1.5.0 or later.

What the seal shows. The hash chain makes changes to stored records detectable. It does not stop someone who can rewrite the app's stored data, including the vendor, from writing a new consistent chain.

The admin page Jira settings > Apps > LedgerSeal settings has four tabs: Approver groups, Signing codes (section 7), Security alerts (section 7) and Approval routes (section 9). Only Jira administrators can use it.

  2. Search in Find Jira groups to grant authority, then choose Add. Remove takes a group off.
  3. Choose Save changes. The page shows "Saved."

12. Data stored and where

  • Where: in the Forge storage of your site, and in one issue property per issue. LedgerSeal sends nothing outside your Atlassian site. With code signing on, it asks Jira to email a signer their own code; Jira sends the email.
  • In Forge storage: the seal state, one record per seal (signer token, role, reason, declaration), the hash-chained history, and the sealed snapshot: field values, comment bodies, attachment details (no file content) and work logs. A reseal deletes the previous snapshot. Approval routes and their versions, and the snapshot that the stages of a running approval sign.
  • Code signing: per person, the party token, the lockout state and a hash-chained history of code requests and attempts; the site's alert log. A code is stored only as a keyed hash, for at most a day. No email address is stored.
  • On the issue: the property ledgerseal-cert with the status, certificate id, snapshot hash and seal date, and on a routed issue the approval state. It holds no personal data. Its status and the approval state can be searched with JQL.
  • People: stored as a pseudonymous token, a keyed hash of the Atlassian account id. One record per person links the token to the account id. No email and no display name is stored. The account ids are reported to Atlassian every week; for a closed account the link is removed and the panel shows "a deleted user".
  • Retention: records stay until the issue is deleted. Deleting an issue deletes its records.

See the privacy policy.

13. Limits

  • Sealed content: at most about 2.6 MB per issue. A larger issue cannot be sealed.
  • Jira reads: at most 5,000 comments, work logs or changelog entries per issue.
  • Reason: 1 to 500 characters. Approver groups: 20.
  • History: 50 entries per page. Up to 50 changed items are stored per change incident.
  • A validator message names at most 5 changed items.
  • Code signing: at most 100 projects. A code works once, for 5 minutes. One code request per 30 seconds and 5 per 15 minutes per person. Lockout after 3 to 10 failures in a row (default 5).
  • A signature in the transition field works once, within 2 minutes.
  • Approval routes: 1 to 8 stages and order groups, 1 to 10 approver groups per stage, a quorum of 1 to 10, and at most 10 gated statuses per stage.

15. Uninstall and data removal

  1. Remove the validator Require Valid LedgerSeal from your workflows first.
  2. A Jira administrator opens Jira settings > Apps > Manage apps and uninstalls LedgerSeal.
  3. Atlassian keeps the app's Forge storage for 28 days and then deletes it: seals, history, snapshots and settings.
  4. The issue property ledgerseal-cert stays on each issue until the issue is deleted.

Keep a copy of any evidence record you need before you uninstall. For other requests, write to privacy@asymm.systems.
